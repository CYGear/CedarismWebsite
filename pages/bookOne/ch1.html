Primi Dies Terrae (The First Days of The Earth) Chapter 1

 In the beginning, there was a Cedar Tree
.1

A divine cedar, magnificent and huge occupying all space
.2

And The Holy Cedar Tree reached out its branches and gave
the earth its form, and created all of the land and the water
and the rest, and saw that they were fit to sustain life
.3

And from this observation The Holy Cedar Tree made the decision
that it should let the land flourish with creatures of all sizes,
so it started a chain
.4

It began the life in the water, unified as one
.5

Naturally the life split off and became its own life,
and each life of its own became vastly different
from each life, and over the millions of years
they became unto their own
.6

And slowly the life came out of the water,
and changed, and some life came up to the trees,
and as the life changed they came down from the trees,
while some stayed in the trees,
and some never went to the trees in the first place,
or left the ocean
.7

But they were all life, no matter two legs or four
.8

And the Lord Cedar saw that its natural
evolutionary order had taken place,
and that it had waited long enough,
and it spoke unto the first tribe
.9

“Take my seeds, and plant them across the land,
and all my child trees and cousin trees shall thrive,
and provide you with food and with life
.10

For without them you will soon surely lose
the ability to breathe,
and lose a source for food
.11

Though you may eat the life that is lesser than you,
the balance between the fruit and the life is necessary,
and you shall not over eat one”
.12

And the humans asked,
“But you are almighty,
divine cedar tree!
.13

Couldn't you use your powers to spread
the seeds yourself?”
.14

And the Lord Cedar replied,
“But it shows obedience to one's creator,
therefore thou shalt spread my children
across the land and thrive from their fruit”
.15

And as commanded, the tribes did.
And the children of The Lord Cedar were
spread all across the land and bore fruit
which the tribe ate
.16

And thus became the trees of the planet earth,
which let the life breathe and flourish
.17
