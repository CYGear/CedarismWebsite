The earliest church meetings were evidently
not the first ones ever recorded, from the
carefully practiced manner in which
they were jotted down.
The original language lays here:

Propheta affirmavit eos magnas visiones a Cedro
accepisse, quae de cultu divino praeciperent;
dixerunt etiam tempus adventurum esse quo
Cedrus rediret. Eo tempore, ut videtur, nos,
qui Cedrum colimus, ad magnum Hortum Sacrum
pergemus, dum alii in viscera terrae igneae
incidentes propter scelera sua peribunt.
Plurima pars sermonis de rebus particularibus
agebat quae tempore reditus Domini Cedri
eventurae essent — velut de arboribus ingentem
scalam formantibus, quae cultores Cedri ad
Hortum Sacrum veherent, ceteris autem relictis
ut poenas luerent. Postremo cenam precationemque
solitas peregimus atque domos nostras rediimus.

Directly translated the text turns into:

The prophet affirmed that they had received
great visions from Cedrus, giving instructions
regarding divine worship; they also said that
a time would come when Cedrus would return.
At that time, it seems, we who worship Cedrus
will journey to the great Sacred Garden, while
others, falling into the fiery bowels of the
earth, will perish because of their wickedness.
Much of the discourse dealt with specific events
expected at the time of Lord Cedrus’s return—such
as trees forming a massive ladder to carry
Cedrus’s worshippers to the Sacred Garden,
while the rest were left behind to pay the
penalty. Finally, we partook of the customary
meal and prayers, and returned to our homes.

This was the earliest surviving church record,
much has been lost as to how the first church
of Cederism fundamentally operated, only some
about the hierarchal systems and worship
procedures is known. All of the known information
on worship procedures was translated into
Ritus Debiti for ease of information to the reader.

It is believed that the Anti-Cederists as
described in Desertio Creatoris destroyed the
first vault of church records in Gens Cedri,
containing the earliest ones before they were
expelled from the holy city.

Another early church document is the basis
for the first chapter of Primi Dies Terrae:

Dominus Cedrus viderat naturalem eius ordinem
evolutionis peractum esse et locutus est ad
primam tribum. "Accipite semina mea et collocate
ea per terram, et omnes pueros meos arborum
et cognatos arborum florent et vobis cibum ac
vitam praebeant. Nam sine eis mox certe
amittetis facultatem respirandi et fontem cibi.
Quamvis vitam minorem quam vos edatis,
aequilibrium inter fructum et vitam necessarium
est, et non supercomedetis unum." "Sed tu
omnipotens es, divina cedrus! Nonne potes uti
tuis viribus ad semina spargenda ipse?" Et
Dominus Cedrus respondit, "Sed ostendit
obedientiam ad creatorem, ideo expansuri estis
pueros meos per terram et prosperabimini ex
fructu eorum." Et iuxta mandatum, tribus
fecerunt. Et filii Domini Cedri per terram
dispersae sunt et fructum tulere quem tribus
edi. Sic factae sunt arbores terrae planetae,
quae vitam respirare et florere sinunt.

Directly translated this becomes:

Lord Cedar saw that the natural order of
evolution had been completed and spoke to the
first tribe: 'Take my seeds and plant them
across the land, so that all my tree children
and their relatives will grow and give you food
and life. Without them, you will soon definitely
lose the ability to breathe and your food source.
Even though they have a shorter life than you,
a balance between fruit and life is necessary,
and don’t overeat one.' 'But you are all-powerful,
divine Cedar! Can’t you use your powers to
scatter the seeds yourself?' And Lord Cedar
replied, 'It shows obedience to the Creator,
so you will spread my children across the land
and prosper from their fruits.' And following
the command, the tribes did as told. And the
children of Lord Cedar scattered across the
land and bore the fruits that the tribes ate.
This is how the trees of the planet Earth were
created, allowing life to breathe and flourish.

Obviously these passages were reworded for
emphasis and coherence, as well as using other
church excerpts for context.
