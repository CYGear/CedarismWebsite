"Primi Dies Terrae"

For the most part Primi Dies Terrae is the most
historically accurate book, since the first,
second, and third generations of Antioch all
kept entire journals for each member,
allowing easy translation and third person
narratives to be constructed through
the eyewitness accounts.

For example, from Lauren’s journal comes
the best account of what happened to
Tyler and Sebastian:
“Hodie Tyler et Sebastianus acerrime inter
se pugnaverunt, nescio qua de causa.
Nemo scit cur id fecerint,
sed pugna fuit vehementissima et letalis!
Tandem Tyler Sebastianum interfecit;
quo interfecto, ipse quoque modo arcano
mortuus est—sed qua de causa,
adhuc ignoramus.”

The literal translation reads as:
“Today Tyler and Sebastian fought each
other fiercely, for some unknown reason.
No one knows why they did it,
but the fight was extremely violent
and deadly! Finally, Tyler killed
Sebastian; once Sebastian was killed,
Tyler himself also died in a mysterious
way—but the cause remains unknown.”

The context of what happened to Tyler
and his mysterious death were added
from a fifth generation church scroll
describing the encounter’s aftermath
in detail: “Dominus Cedar raro rebus
humanis se immiscere volebat,
sed tandem statuit satis damni iam
illatum esse. Nam Tyler mortuus erat
atque in agris iuxta Sebastianum
iacebat; quem Dominus Cedar sustulit
et in tempus futurum transtulit.
Dixitque Dominus Cedar: "Facinus
nefarium admisisti quod non praevideram.
Scelus tuum crudeliter punietur,
sicut facta tua crudelia fuerunt."
Tum Dominus Cedar poenam aeternam constituit,
quam omnes malefici post mortem subirent.
Itaque Tyler reliquum aeternitatis intra
arborem degit, ibi inclusus atque intolerabili
pollinis irritatione vexatus.”

Directly translated, this becomes:
“Lord Cedar rarely wished to involve himself
in human affairs, but at last he decided
that enough harm had been done.
For Tyler was dead and lay in the fields
beside Sebastian; Lord Cedar lifted him up
and transported him into the future.
And Lord Cedar said: "You have committed a heinous
deed that I did not foresee.
Your crime shall be punished cruelly,
just as your deeds were cruel."
Then Lord Cedar decreed an eternal punishment
that all evildoers would undergo after death.
And so Tyler spent the rest of eternity inside a tree,
confined there and tormented by the unbearable
irritation of pollen.”

This is a near exact description and translation
of the more direct and easier to read version
presented in chapter 3 of Primi Dies Terrae.
Only a few words such as moving Tyler
into the Future make little sense,
and were rectified based upon sense
and other sources.

Note: Throughout the texts,
Cedrus uses He/it pronouns,
but literal translations often move towards
He/Him or It/Its. The combination of sources
leads to the combination of different
references towards Cedrus’s identity and its pronouns.
